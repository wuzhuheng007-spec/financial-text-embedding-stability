# M7 research note — descriptive engineering evidence

**Scope.** Five saved, original 10-K Item 1 Business sections (AAPL, MSFT, AMZN, GOOGL, META); one fixed pretrained `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` revision `1110a243fdf4706b3f48f1d95db1a4f5529b4d41`; 128/192 nonoverlapping body-token chunks. This is a post hoc mechanism check prompted by M6 results, not a preregistered test or formal financial study. The complete inputs, configuration, hashes and computations are in `artifacts/m7/20261024T223714Z-tail-bm25-10q/input_output_manifest.json` and the [M7 engineering report](2026-10-25_M7_mechanism_and_10q.md).

**Supported observations.** The saved M6 equal and body-token-weighted vectors were reproduced before new interventions. At chunk length 192, MSFT's 41-token last chunk receives normalized weight `1/45` under equal pooling versus `41/8489` under body-token weighting. Its second neighbor changes from META to AMZN (GOOGL remains first); the other four companies' Top-2 membership does not change. The 128-token Top-2 sets do not change. Exact margins, all company rows, removed text-span hashes and cosine matrices are in `tail_v2/tail_weight_diagnostics.json`; removing a final chunk is an omission diagnostic, not a proposed representation method.

For every saved document and both chunk sizes, each nonfinal chunk has exactly `L` body tokens. Thus body-token weights `[L,…,L,t]` and tail-only weights `[1,…,1,t/L]` differ by one common positive factor. With identical normalized chunk vectors and the same mean/document-L2 order, they produce the same document direction. This algebraic identity is **one condition represented twice**, not an independent empirical finding. It would need rechecking if nonfinal chunks were short, overlap or normalization changed, or special/padding tokens were counted as body tokens.

On the same five-text lexical fit, BM25 `k1=1.2` and `b=0,0.75,1` alter raw term weights and directed query-to-document scores. The directed score sums document weights for unique query terms and is generally asymmetric. The separately named BM25 term-weight cosine first L2 normalizes the document vectors; it is symmetric. At `b=0` versus `b=1`, the cosine upper-triangle ordering is unchanged in these ten dependent company pairs while numerical cosines change (maximum absolute off-diagonal difference `0.001899`). Directed scores change more in their own units and the full peer order changes for AMZN, GOOGL and MSFT. The best-scalar residuals are nonzero for all five real documents. See `bm25/bm25_normalization_diagnostics.json` and `bm25/synthetic_software_controls.json`; the latter contains formula tests only, never company findings.

**Unresolved interpretation.** The data show sensitivity under these chosen settings, not whether a changed embedding is semantically better. A stable cosine order does not make length correction ineffective, and a changed directed score does not establish better retrieval. Text length, term-frequency mix, short tails, narrow rank margins and section content are plausible contributors; this run does not identify their causal shares. Five related firms, one filing section per firm, one model revision and one lexical fit boundary cannot support universal stability claims or significance tests treating company pairs as independent. The single AAPL 10-Q MD&A engineering representation is a separate section; human boundary/table review is still pending. No return prediction, financial incremental-value test or point-in-time validity check was run.

**Next evaluation gate.** First obtain recorded human decisions on SEC section boundaries and readable tables. Then predefine a larger, section-consistent filing sample across firms and periods, missingness/revision rules, independent models or training variation where relevant, and an external semantic/retrieval criterion before comparing representational quality. Any optional financial task also needs a frozen M5 decision time, text-availability policy, chronological split, price-only and lexical controls, and metrics. M7 does not nominate a winning chunk length, pooling rule or BM25 `b`.
